# Strivo Opportunities — Marketing Brief

Use this to prime ChatGPT (or any tool) before asking it to draft posts, reels, ads, or email copy about this feature.

## What it is

Opportunities is a tab inside Strivo (the career-memory app) that shows a person real, live job openings that Strivo believes they're genuinely a fit for — without them ever typing a search query, picking a city, or filling in a filter form.

Strivo already knows a person's career story because they've been recording it as memories (and can upload a resume). Opportunities is what that knowledge is *for*: instead of a generic job board where you search and dig through hundreds of irrelevant listings, Strivo already knows your function, your industry, your seniority, and your city — and just shows you the roles that make sense, with the single best match called out up front.

## The core insight (use this as the hook)

Every job board on earth makes you describe yourself to it — keywords, filters, dropdowns — every single time you search. Strivo already knows who you are, because you told it, one real memory at a time. Opportunities is the payoff: your job search happens automatically, in the background, because the app already understands your career.

This is the "why Strivo, not just ChatGPT / a job board" argument for this feature specifically — lead with it.

## How it works, in plain terms

- A person records memories in Strivo about their actual work — projects, wins, decisions, what they did.
- From that (plus an optional resume upload), Strivo quietly builds a real picture of them: what kind of role they're ready for, what industry they belong to, how senior they are, and where they're based.
- Strivo continuously watches the real job market and matches genuinely open roles against that picture.
- Every match comes with a one-line, specific reason it's a fit — tied to something real from their own career, not a generic "great opportunity for growth" line.
- Matches are shown in tiers (Strong fit / Good fit / Worth exploring), with the best one visually called out as the top match.
- A person can tap "Fit" or "Not a fit" on any card, and Strivo remembers that and keeps refining what it shows them.
- Every single listing routes straight to the employer's own official application page — never a job-board redirect, a third-party portal, or a dead link loop. That's a deliberate trust choice, not a default.
- There's nothing to fill in. No search bar, no filters, no "tell us what you're looking for" form — that was a deliberate product decision: the whole point is that a person shouldn't have to describe themselves to Strivo when Strivo already knows.

## Positioning angles worth trying

- **"Your job search runs on autopilot."** You don't search for jobs, jobs find you — because you already told Strivo who you are.
- **Loss aversion, not feature-listing.** Frame it as what someone's missing by not having this — being matched to roles they'd never have found by searching, or not knowing about a strong-fit opening that's already live right now — rather than a dry list of capabilities.
- **Contrast with the generic job-board experience.** Everyone's tired of typing the same keywords into the same five job sites. Strivo is the anti-job-board: it already read your career, so it skips the part where you have to explain yourself.
- **Tie it back to memory-recording as the habit.** The better someone's memory history, the sharper their matches get — this gives a real, concrete reason to keep recording memories regularly, not just "for the resume later."
- **Show, don't just tell.** A short before/after or a screen recording of "here's what your Opportunities tab looks like the moment you open it" tends to land harder than describing the mechanism.

## Voice and tone

- Keep it personal and specific, not corporate-AI-speak. Avoid "leverage," "unlock your potential," "powered by advanced AI," and similar generic phrasing.
- Lead with the outcome for the person, not the technology. Nobody cares that it's "AI-matched" — they care that it found them a real job they'd actually want, with zero effort.
- No "free app" framing as the headline — center what someone's missing out on by not using it, not the price.
- Confident but not hypey. Strivo has a real, working feature here — no need to oversell it with superlatives.

## Do not include in any public content

- No mention of any specific job-data provider, AI model/vendor, database, hosting, or other backend/infrastructure detail.
- No admin/internal tooling, API keys, partner IDs, or anything about how matching is technically implemented.
- No reference to any past bug, outage, or incident.
- Keep it strictly about the product and the value to the person using it.
